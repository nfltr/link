## Bugs and findings

### B1. TestDrainDuringHandshake is a racy test (peerlink/node_test.go:509), test only

**Fixed** on main by 4d5be91 (see the follow-up of 2026-10-21).

The failure is `node_test.go:509: peerlink: open to "server": dial tcp 127.0.0.1:PORT: connect: connection refused`, at 0.00 s.

| condition | failures |
|---|---|
| quiet, ×200 (§3) | 3 / 200 |
| contended, 6 × 200 (§4) | 1 / 1200 |
| narrow repro, alone, instrumented binaries | 2 / 1500 and 3 / 300 |

The WIP report saw it too (Docker arm64, 4 of 6 narrow runs).

No seed: the test is unseeded and `-test.shuffle` was off.

Repro: `go test -race -count=300 -run '^TestDrainDuringHandshake$' ./peerlink`,
repeated until a run fails (1 to 4 runs of 300 here).

Cause, from two instrumented reruns (`drain-during-handshake-failure.txt`):

1. The client's first dial succeeds, and the server's handshake reaches
   `ServedPeer`, which blocks. The test then starts `Drain`, which closes
   the listener.
2. On `close(release)` the server registers the connection, sees it is
   draining and sends GOAWAY.
3. The client's `Pool.Open` had not yet sent its open on the connection it
   dialled (`Client` returns once the server's reply is in, and the server
   writes that reply *before* `ServedPeer`). Its `OpenChain` then fails
   with `link: connection going away` (goingAway=true).
4. `Pool.Open` picks again (pool.go:200) and dials. The listener is
   closed, so the dial is refused. The stack of the second dial is in the
   file.

The test says "a session the peer opened on a connection whose handshake
ended as Drain began": it assumes the client's open precedes the GOAWAY,
which nothing orders. link behaves as documented here (no new session on a
connection going away; a draining Node refuses). Severity: flaky test, low.

### B2. TestPool hangs instead of failing (v2_test.go:523 to 545), test only

**Fixed** on main by 07d7b34 (see the follow-up of 2026-10-26).

Contended shard 4 stopped at `=== RUN TestPool` (its first pass) at
10:20:08. At 12:20 it had used 30 s of CPU in 2 h, and I dumped its
goroutines with SIGQUIT (`testpool-hang-goroutines.txt`):

- `TestPool` is at v2_test.go:545 (`<-drained`).
- `Pool.Drain` (started at v2_test.go:527) is at pool.go:385, waiting for an
  old connection's `Done`.
- One server-side `v2echo` session (`Session.WriteTo` → `bytequeue.PopBatch`)
  is still open on that connection, waiting for bytes.

Repro: `go test -race -c -o link.test .` once, then, under load (4 × `yes > /dev/null` on 4 CPUs):
`./link.test -test.count=5000 -test.v -test.run '^TestPool$' -test.timeout 15m`.
4 of 4 workers hung (the test timed out, every time at v2_test.go:545 → 527)
after 1, 9, 34 and 69 passes: about 4 hangs in 117 runs at load 19.
Deterministic: a 100 ms sleep in `Pool.dial` before it registers the 4th
connection (temporary, reverted) makes the first run hang.

Cause:

1. The test waits `waitUntil(accepted.Load() == 4)` and then opens `s`.
2. `accepted` counts the *server's* handshake, which ends before the
   client's `Pool.dial` has added the replacement connection to `p.conns`,
   and before `Drain` has sent GOAWAY on the old ones.
3. So `p.Open` can put `s` on one of the 3 old connections. That
   connection is then GOAWAYed with `s` open on it.
4. The test never closes `s` before `<-drained`, and `Drain(context.Background())`
   has no deadline, so it waits forever.

This matches `Pool.Drain`'s doc (old connections close "once its open
sessions ended, at ctx's deadline at the latest"). Severity: test hang. It
costs a whole `-timeout` (8 h here, 10 min by default) and the shard's
remaining passes.

Severity: flaky under 3.5× CPU oversubscription. I'd keep an eye on it but
not block on it.

## Follow-up, 2026-10-26: B4, quiclink Drain skipped a connection being dialled

Symptom: on the Linux runner (a065109) `TestOpenTowardDrainingPeer`
(peerlink/quiclink) waited for b's GOAWAY until the package's 10m
timeout; 40629e5 bounded the wait at 10s. The goroutine dump held a's
listener only: no connection between a and b, b's Drain returned.

Root cause (library, quiclink v0.5.0 `dial`, quiclink.go:476 and
`Drain`, quiclink.go:736): Drain snapshotted the Node's connections; a
connection whose dial had finished its handshake but was not yet in
`dialled` (here `Connect`'s, by `keep`) was not in the snapshot, and
`dial` then saw `draining` and closed it with `CodeDone`, no GOAWAY. The
peer had already taken it and opened a session on it, so it never saw
GOAWAY and its next `Open` waited for a connection until its ctx instead
of failing with `link.ErrGoAway`. The window is a few instructions wide
(600 runs, 3 × `GOMAXPROCS=1` in parallel at load 9, did not hit it).

Invariant: a draining Node sends GOAWAY on every connection a peer may
have opened sessions on, one it was still dialling when Drain began
included, before that connection closes.

Fix: `dial` counts itself in flight; Drain waits for those dials (or its
ctx) before it snapshots, so their connections are drained with the
rest; a dial that lands after a Drain's deadline closes its connection
with `CodeDrainDeadline`.

Guard: `TestDrainDuringDialSendsGoAway` holds b's dial between its
handshake and the Node taking it until b's Drain began: 3/3 fail at the
10s bound before the fix, pass after. Stress after the fix, `-race`:
these two and `TestDrainDeadlineCutIsDrainDeadline` `-count=200` quiet
and 3 × `GOMAXPROCS=1 -count=200` in parallel (load ≈ 9 on 10 CPUs):
pass. The package `-count=20` (load 6 to 30 from other work): every
drain, chain and conformance test passed; one `TestThroughputAfterBurstTheOtherWay`
fell under its floor (2.19 MB/s, want 4; no drain or dial in its path),
10/10 alone after.
